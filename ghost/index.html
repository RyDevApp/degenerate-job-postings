What Are Ghost Jobs?

The emergence of the "Ghost Job Economy" represents one of the most significant yet underappreciated challenges confronting American workers and policymakers. Nearly one in three job postings—approximately 30% of all vacancies—never result in an actual hire. This phantom gap has held steady at 28-38% monthly since 2021, fundamentally distorting perceptions of labor market health.

📊 Key Finding: In June 2025, employers advertised 7.4 million positions but filled only 5.2 million, leaving over 2.2 million roles that existed entirely on paper.

Historical Evolution

Understanding how we arrived at this crisis requires examining the dramatic shift in labor market dynamics since 2021.

2010-2019: The Era of Alignment

Job openings and hiring moved in tandem with remarkable consistency. Growth from 2M to 7M positions annually, with the gap between openings and hires rarely exceeding 10%. The labor market operated with relative transparency and efficiency.

2020: Pandemic Disruption

Both job openings and hiring collapsed simultaneously, maintaining the traditional ratio. While disruptive, the balanced decline initially preserved labor market equilibrium.

2021-2022: The Breaking Point

Job postings surged to above 11 million while hiring remained flat at 6-7 million. As many as 38% of all postings failed to result in hires. Employer behavior fundamentally changed in ways that would permanently alter the relationship between job advertisements and actual employment outcomes.

2023-2025: The New Normal

Even as economic conditions cooled, the "ghost job rate" remained stubbornly elevated at 28-32% monthly. This persistent gap suggests structural changes in how businesses approach recruitment, talent management, and workforce planning.

Why Do Companies Post Ghost Jobs?

Understanding the causes reveals that the phenomenon results from neither a single cause nor entirely intentional deception by employers.

🎯 Building Talent Pools

50% of HR professionals cite this reason. Companies view job posting as continuous recruitment activity, creating pools of pre-screened candidates for rapid deployment when business conditions improve.

📅 Seasonal & Pipeline Planning

48% of employers maintain postings for seasonal hiring needs and anticipated future growth, particularly in retail, hospitality, and education sectors.

📊 Structural Labor Shortages

35% of unfilled positions reflect genuine labor shortages. Healthcare, education, and tech sectors post real positions but cannot find qualified workers.

⏸️ Administrative Delays

Budget freezes, internal approvals, and shifting strategic priorities can derail hiring even after positions have been posted.

📈 Growth Signaling

25% of companies post positions partly to convey an impression of growth and expansion to employees, investors, and competitors.

🔍 Market Research

Some employers use job postings as market research to understand salary expectations, required skills, and competitive compensation practices without immediate hiring intent.

The Devastating Impact on Job Seekers

The consequences of ghost jobs extend far beyond wasted time. For millions of American workers, encounters with ghost positions inflict significant psychological harm and economic losses.

Mental Health Crisis

  • 72% of job seekers report that the employment process negatively impacts their mental health
  • 68% of job seekers report that ghosting and the job search process hurts their mental health
  • Some experience depression lasting weeks or months after being ghosted post-interview
  • Psychological impact particularly severe for underrepresented candidates (66% vs 59% for white candidates)
"I invested so much time and effort, and when I received no feedback, it left me in a state of distress. I began to question myself: What is wrong with me? Will I ever find work again?"
— Anonymous Job Seeker

The Ghosting Epidemic

  • 61% of job seekers report being ghosted after interviews (up 9% since early 2024)
  • 76% of recruiters have been ghosted by candidates
  • 44% of job seekers admit to ghosting employers
  • 34% of Gen Z workers have engaged in "career catfishing"—accepting positions only to vanish on their first day

Economic Costs

  • Average cost per hire: $4,700
  • When candidates ghost after accepting: 22% never show up for their first day
  • Time investment per ghosted application: 47 hours
  • Estimated annual economic loss: $2.5 billion in lost productivity

Policy Solutions & Future Directions

Recognizing the severity of these issues, policymakers and industry leaders are proposing reforms to increase transparency and accountability in hiring.

🏛️ Proposed Legislation

Truth in Job Advertising & Accountability Act (TJAAA) would require employers to disclose hiring intent, provide salary bands, and remove filled positions within specified timeframes. Enforcement authority granted to Department of Labor and FTC.

🗳️ State-Level Action

New Jersey and California have introduced state-level legislation requiring that postings specify whether roles are actively open and mandating removal of filled positions within defined timeframes.

🔍 Platform Transparency

Job platforms beginning implementation of detection systems to identify positions likely posted without genuine hiring intent, with automated removal of outdated postings.

🤝 Employer Accountability

Industry standards emphasizing ethical recruitment practices, respectful candidate communication, and transparent hiring timelines to restore trust in labor markets.

Conclusion

The Ghost Job Economy represents one of the most significant yet underappreciated challenges confronting American workers and labor market functionality. The persistence of a 28-32% gap between job postings and actual hires since 2021 reveals a fundamental misalignment between what employers advertise and what they deliver.

While some unfilled positions reflect genuine labor shortages in specific sectors—a real problem requiring distinct solutions—other ghost jobs represent deliberate or negligent employer behavior that wastes millions of workers' time, damages mental health, and distorts the economic data upon which critical policy decisions depend.

The solution requires multifaceted approaches: regulatory measures ensuring transparency, platform accountability for outdated postings, cultural shifts prioritizing candidate respect, and structural investments in workforce development to address genuine labor shortages. Until job postings more accurately represent real opportunities, millions of Americans will continue investing time and emotional energy in chasing positions that exist only on paper.

In an economy increasingly defined by anxiety and diminished trust, restoring integrity to the job search process represents not merely a matter of courtesy, but a fundamental requirement for healthy labor market function.
